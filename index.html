I am a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modeler and scientist, hydraulic modeler, and flood scientist, as well as a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Civil Engineering at the University of Ottawa working with Prof. Majid Mohammadian. My research combines computational modeling, laboratory experiments, and data-driven methods to study environmental flows, flood processes, and pollutant transport through aquatic environments.

My current focus is the transport and fate of microplastics in water systems: how particle properties such as size, density, shape, and buoyancy interact with turbulence, shear, and aquatic vegetation to control where microplastics travel and where they accumulate in rivers, channels, and coastal waters.

Current Research

GPU-Accelerated Eulerian–Lagrangian Modeling of Microplastic Transport

A computational framework coupling vegetation-aware RANS hydrodynamics (OpenFOAM) with a GPU-based Lagrangian particle solver (Python/CuPy) to simulate microplastic transport and dispersion in vegetated open-channel flows, validated against laboratory experiments. Manuscript in preparation with Prof. Majid Mohammadian.

Research Areas

Microplastic Transport & Dispersion

Force-based Lagrangian modeling of microplastic particles in turbulent open-channel flows, including drag, buoyancy, added mass, lift, and stochastic turbulent dispersion, with emphasis on vegetation effects, breakthrough curves, and environmental-scale prediction.

Environmental Hydraulics of Water & Wastewater Systems

Hydraulic efficiency and pollutant transport in waste stabilization ponds, retention ponds, sedimentation basins, and chlorine contact tanks, including thermal and wind effects on treatment performance.

Buoyant Jets & Desalination Discharges

Numerical and experimental study of dense and buoyant jets: desalination brine outfalls, mixing under crossflow and stratification, LIF measurements, and multifidelity Bayesian prediction of dilution.

Turbulence & Data-Driven Modeling

Large eddy simulation of turbidity currents and treatment units, and machine-learning surrogates for environmental prediction, bridging high-fidelity simulation and practical engineering models.

Software & Tools

Ariake Survey Instrument

A Bayesian instrument for microplastic surveys of the Ariake Sea. Place a few stations, enter the concentrations you measured, and it reconstructs the whole suspended field with its uncertainty, names the governing process, says where to sample next, and traces the plastic back to the rivers it came from. Trained on 1,304 simulated years of the bay; unlike the two visualizations below, every number comes from the real campaign.
