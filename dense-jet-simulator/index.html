Dense Jet Simulator

An interactive demonstration of an inclined negatively buoyant (dense) jet, the flow configuration typical of desalination brine outfalls. The centerline is computed with a simplified Lagrangian integral model (shear and crossflow-forced entrainment, buoyancy-flux evolution, optional linear stratification), and particles ride the solution to show the rising jet, the terminal rise, the descending limb, and the bottom density current that spreads after bed impact.

⚠ Illustrative demonstration only. This animation is a simplified, qualitative visualization intended for teaching and outreach. It is not based on real scientific data, has not been validated against experiments, and must not be used for research, design, or regulatory purposes. What to try: compare the classic 30°, 45°, and 60° inclined-discharge presets and watch the terminal rise and return point move; add crossflow to bend the plume downstream; switch on stratification to suppress the rise height; lower the water depth until the plume interacts with the free surface. The metrics panel reports the densimetric Froude number and the normalized rise height zₜ/(D·Frd), the standard nondimensional groups used in the dense-jet literature.
