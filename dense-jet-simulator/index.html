Interactive simulator desalination brine discharges

How a dense jet rises, falls and spreads.

An inclined negatively buoyant jet, the flow typical of desalination brine outfalls: terminal rise, return point, bed impact and the density current that spreads along the bed.

Open the simulator → Classic version

Fig. 1 A laboratory tank, drawn live. The dashed red line marks the terminal rise and the red ring the bed impact.

I

A simplified integral model

The centerline is computed with a simplified Lagrangian integral model: shear and crossflow-forced entrainment, buoyancy-flux evolution and an optional linear stratification. Particles ride the solution to show the rising jet, the terminal rise, the descending limb and the bottom density current that spreads after bed impact.

The metrics panel reports the densimetric Froude number and the normalized rise height zt/(D·Frd), the standard nondimensional groups used in the dense-jet literature.
