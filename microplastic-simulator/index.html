Microplastic Journey

A schematic, side-view visualization of how microplastic particles travel through a river and into a lake. Particles are advected by the flow, mixed by turbulence, and trapped by aquatic vegetation. Depending on their polymer density, size, and biofouling, they float at the surface, stay suspended, or settle to the bed, where river shear can resuspend them. A live fate budget tracks where every released particle ends up.

⚠ Illustrative demonstration only. This animation is a simplified, conceptual visualization intended for teaching and outreach. It is not based on real scientific data, has not been validated against experiments or field observations, and must not be used for research, design, or regulatory purposes.

What to try: release pure polyethylene and watch it raft along the surface until biofouling makes it heavy enough to sink; switch to PVC and see it settle quickly in the river reach; increase vegetation density and compare how much material the canopy traps; trigger a storm event to flush trapped and settled particles back into suspension and watch the fate budget redistribute; then let the lake do what lakes do: act as a long-term sink for whatever arrives.
