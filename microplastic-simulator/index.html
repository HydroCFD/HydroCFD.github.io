Research project on microplastic transport

Following every microplastic through the water.

A GPU-accelerated three-dimensional Lagrangian numerical model for microplastic transport

Danial Goodarzi, Abdolmajid Mohammadian, Abolghasem Pilechi and Atsuhiko Isobe

Explore the model → Watch the simulations

A meandering river, drawn live. Particles drift, grow a biofilm, break apart and settle.

I

One particle, one thread, the whole water column

Microplastics do not stay the same while they travel. Algae grow on them, they break into smaller pieces, and their size and density change on the way from a river to the sea. These changes decide whether a particle floats, stays in suspension or settles on the bed.

This solver follows each particle through the three-dimensional flow field of a hydrodynamic model. The transport and the transformation of every particle are calculated together on the graphics card, with one GPU thread for each particle.

  1. Ray tracing on prisms

    Particles move from face to face through the triangular prism mesh of the hydrodynamic model. The hosting prism is always known, so no search is needed.

  2. Drift-corrected random walk

    Turbulent mixing is represented by a random walk with a drift correction, so particles do not collect artificially where the diffusivity changes.

  3. Settling in real water

    Rise and settling velocities use the local density and viscosity of the water, which vary with salinity and temperature.

  4. Bed exchange

    Particles settle on a moving bed and return to the water when the flow is strong enough to lift them again.

  5. Beaching and washout

    Particles carried onto drying shores are stranded, and they can be washed back into the water when the tide returns.

  6. Biofilm growth

    Algae attach and grow on each particle. The film changes its size and density, and a floating particle can start to sink.

  7. Weighted fragmentation

    When a particle breaks, it becomes smaller and represents more fragments. Mass is conserved and the number of computational particles stays constant.

  8. Written for the GPU

    Every particle is independent, so thousands of GPU threads advance thousands of particles at the same time. A CPU version runs the same physics.

II

What happens to one piece of plastic

Scroll to follow a single particle as the model sees it.

Fragments representedw = 1
1

Released into the flow

A small polyethylene sphere is lighter than water. The flow carries it downstream, and turbulence moves it up and down in the water column. The model follows it through each prism of the mesh.

2

A biofilm grows

Algal cells meet the particle by Brownian motion, by the shear of the flow and as the particle moves through the water. They attach and grow in colonies, and the colonies join into a layer that thickens around the sphere.

3

It starts to sink

The film is denser than the plastic. Once the fouled particle becomes heavier than the surrounding water, it leaves the surface and settles through the water column.

4

It breaks apart

Weathering and stress break the particle into smaller spheres. In the model, the computational particle becomes smaller and represents more fragments, instead of creating new particles. Each fragment keeps its biofilm.

5

The film weighs more on a fragment

A break keeps the number of algal cells on each unit of surface. A small sphere has less plastic behind each unit of surface than its parent, so the same film forms a larger share of the fragment. Its effective density rises, and a fragment can sink even when its parent could not.

6

It reaches the bed

The fragments settle on the bed and follow it as it moves. A strong current can lift them back into the water, and the journey continues.

III

Plastic breaks up, the particle count does not explode

If every fragment became a new computational particle, the number of particles would double again and again until the memory runs out. The cost of the simulation would then depend on how fast plastic breaks, not on the accuracy that the map needs.

The model uses weighted particles instead, an idea taken from the super-droplet method of cloud physics. Each computational particle stands for w identical fragments. A break makes the fragments smaller and multiplies w, so the represented mass is conserved exactly and the number of computational particles stays the same.

IV

Ray tracing through the mesh of the flow model

The solver reads the three-dimensional velocity field of the hydrodynamic model on its own mesh of triangular prisms. For each particle, it finds which face of the hosting prism is reached first, moves the particle to that face and passes it to the neighbouring prism.

  • No interpolation onto a regular grid and no search for the hosting cell.
  • The same tracing is used for advection and for the random walk.
  • Particles are sorted so that neighbouring threads read neighbouring parts of the mesh.
V

A journey through the meander bends

A synthetic meandering river with helical secondary flow. The 3D view compares transport alone, biofilm growth, fragmentation and both together. The plan and side views follow microplastics of 0.1, 0.3 and 1 mm through the TELEMAC-3D flow while a biofilm grows on them.
