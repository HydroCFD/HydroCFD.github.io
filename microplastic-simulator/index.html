Research project on microplastic transport

Following every microplastic through the water.

A GPU-accelerated three-dimensional Lagrangian numerical model for microplastic transport

Danial Goodarzi, Abdolmajid Mohammadian, Abolghasem Pilechi and Atsuhiko Isobe

Explore the model → Watch the simulations

I

A three-dimensional Lagrangian solver for microplastic transport

The size, the density and the shape of a microplastic particle determine the drag and the settling velocity, and hence whether the microplastic remains in suspension or is deposited. These properties change during the transport of microplastics in water systems, as a biofilm grows on the particle and increases its size and density, and as the microplastic breaks into smaller fragments under weathering and mechanical stress.

This study develops a three-dimensional Lagrangian solver for the unstructured prism meshes of coastal hydrodynamic models, with particle transport calculated in parallel on a graphics processing unit (GPU). The solver includes a drift-corrected random walk, a settling velocity calculated from the density and viscosity of the ambient water, and models for biofilm growth and fragmentation, and each component is verified against an analytical solution or against reference values from the literature.

1

Release and transport

The microplastic is released from a point source and starts without biofilm. In each hydrodynamic step, it is traced through the triangular prism cells of the hydrodynamic mesh by ray tracing, first by advection and then by a separate random displacement that represents turbulent diffusion.

2

Biofilm growth

Algae attach to the particle at a rate that is the sum of three contributions, from Brownian motion, from the shear of the flow and from the difference in settling velocity. The attached algae grow at a rate that depends on the light they receive, and they are lost by grazing and respiration. The film is limited to a thickness of 30 µm and to an effective diameter of 1.1 times the diameter of the microplastic, and the smaller of the two limits applies, which is 15 µm for this particle. The film remains near 9 µm, since the growth of the attached cells is balanced by the losses.

3

Change of the buoyancy

The fouled particle is treated as a mixture of plastic and biofilm, and its effective density is the volume weighted mean of the two densities. The density of this particle exceeds the density of the water for the first time after 1.6 days and then every day between about 13:00 and 19:00. At 1010 cells/m3, the particles at the surface that sink have diameters between 0.16 and 0.63 mm, and at 109 cells/m3 the particles of all diameters remain buoyant. A particle mixed over the water column sinks later, because the light decreases with depth.

4

Fragmentation with weighted particles

A fragmentation event reduces the size of the physical particle and increases the multiplicity w of the computational particle, so that the represented mass is conserved exactly and the number of computational particles remains constant. With the coarse grained fractal cascade, each event replaces the parent by 2.26 fragments with a mass fraction of 0.442, so the diameter decreases by the factor 0.762. The position and the polymer density remain unchanged, and the physical particles of one computational particle have the same position, so the fragments are drawn apart here only so that they can be seen, in a rounded number. Three events are shown close together. At a background rate of 10−6 s−1, an event occurs on average every 12 days, and fragmentation is applied in the same way to suspended, settled and beached particles, down to a smallest fragment size of 1 µm.

5

Attached algal cells at a fragmentation event

The attached algal cells of the parent are shared among the fragments in proportion to their mass, so an event neither creates nor removes cells, and the total number of attached cells and the effective density remain unchanged at the event. The fracture faces, 24 % of the surface of a fragment, are treated as clean surfaces that are colonised afterwards through the attachment term. A fragment therefore carries a thinner film than its parent, with a mean thickness of 9 µm at 0.30 mm, 7 µm at 0.23 mm, 5 µm at 0.17 mm and 4 µm at 0.13 mm. The fragments of this particle remain close to the density of the water, and fragments smaller than about 0.015 mm are denser than the water at almost every hour. The fragment in the close-up is enlarged.

6

Bed exchange, beaching and washout

A particle that reaches the bed with a downward settling velocity is marked as settled and is kept at the bed elevation, so it follows a moving bed until the local flow speed exceeds its critical erosion velocity and it is resuspended. The fragments of this particle below 0.14 mm are denser than the water during at most 4 % of the time. A particle is marked as beached when the water depth of its cell falls below the drying threshold, and it returns to the water with a refloating probability given by the washout rate.

III

Number of computational particles during fragmentation

If the model created a new computational particle for every fragment, the number of particles would grow without limit and exceed the available memory, and it would be determined by the fragmentation rate rather than by the accuracy required of the map.

Fragmentation is therefore represented with weighted computational particles, following the super droplet method. Each computational particle represents w identical physical particles, where w is the multiplicity. In contrast to previous microplastic models, in which fragmentation is a transfer of mass between fixed size classes, the scheme applies fragmentation to each Lagrangian particle during its transport.

IV

Implementation on GPUs

For each of the five faces of the hosting prism, the time at which the particle would reach the face is calculated from the centroid and the normal of the face and from the particle velocity, and the particle is moved to the face that is reached first. If this face is an internal face, the velocity is interpolated again from the nodes of the face and the neighbouring prism becomes the hosting prism.

  • The hosting prism changes only when the particle crosses a face, so no search is required during the simulation.
  • The same tracing procedure is used for the advection and for the random walk.
  • The particles in suspension are sorted by hosting prism, so that the GPU threads executed together read the same part of the mesh.
V

Synthetic meandering river with an instantaneous release

The channel has meander bends, a cross section that is deepest near the outer bank of each bend and the helical secondary flow of the bends, with the flow calculated with TELEMAC-3D. Microplastics of 950 kg/m3 with diameters of 0.1, 0.3 and 1 mm are released at once and followed for three days, with biofilm growth at 2×1010 cells/m3.

VI

Impact of biofilm growth and fragmentation in the Ariake Sea

Microplastics of 950 kg/m3 with diameters of 0.1, 0.3 and 1 mm are released from fourteen locations around the Ariake Sea, Japan, every hour of 2023, with the flow field simulated with TELEMAC-3D. The four simulations differ only in the active transformation processes, and the outputs are 3.5 days apart.
